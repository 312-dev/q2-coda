# Vamp OS: the roadmap

A plan, not shipped code. Vamp OS is the end goal the owner set on 2026-11-26 ("why not decompile it and build our own from ground up following same principles but with higher quality components like open source software as discussed + the new UI engine etc?", then "VampOP it is."): every userspace piece of the Q2 is Vamp's own code or built from open source, on Shanling's kernel and kernel modules, which stay theirs. Today's Vamp patches Shanling's `demo` in place ([architecture](architecture.md)); Vamp OS has no `demo`.

The principle that governs every choice below, the owner's, verbatim:

> i think the critical thing to keep in mind is not to bound ourselves to the same limitations we dont have to anymore whether it omesto the UI framework or other features... we now have more control and should provide a flexible foundation/OS for others to use.

So **parity with stock is the floor, not the ceiling**, none of stock's limits carries over by default ([What does not carry over](#what-does-not-carry-over)), and Vamp OS is designed as a platform other people build on ([Platform](#platform)), with a milestone early in the order that makes it usable by them.

Each statement says how it is known where that matters: **measured** (on a Q2 or in a test, with its source), **read** (from a doc, a source file or the stock image's file list), **inferred**, or **needs a Q2**. Three pieces are in progress on branches of their own and are cited by path until they land: the UI (`docs/ui-rewrite.md` on `feat/render-engine`), BlueZ (`docs/bluez.md` on `feat/bluez-own`) and bluez-alsa (`docs/bluealsa.md` on `feat/bluealsa-own`, not written yet on 2026-11-26).

## Verdict

**Feasible, as a staged replacement, and worth doing in this order.** The reasons, shortest first:

- The precedent holds. `q2player` replaced `hciplayer` whole, behind a switch, by parity checklist ([player-plan](player-plan.md)). The UI spike repeats the result where it matters most: on a Q2, on 9,198 songs, a slow wheel tick costs `q2ui` 5.8 to 6.0 ms of CPU against `demo`'s 113 to 114 ms, about a nineteenth, with 3.5 MB of anonymous memory against 19.8 MB (measured 2026-11-26, `docs/ui-rewrite.md` "Measured on a Q2").
- Nothing in userspace is out of reach. Every stock program and library either has an upstream project, is already Vamp's, or is a duty of `demo` whose hardware interface is a file, an ioctl or a D-Bus call ([Inventory](#inventory)). What is not ours and cannot be is below userspace: the kernel, its 24 modules, the radio's firmware files, the MCU's firmware, U-Boot and the recovery.
- The cost is the application, not the engine: 90 stock pages, 22 dialogs and about 122,000 lines of payload written against stock's widgets (read: `docs/ui-rewrite.md`). The total is about 180 to 265 orchestration items, after the owner's [scope](#scope) took about 14 to 22 out ([Effort](#effort)), and the services at the end are paced by sessions with a Q2 on a desk, not by writing.
- The legal exposure shrinks at a milestone that can be named ([M5](#m5-the-vamp-os-base-and-the-installer)), not at the end.

The odds, with reasons, are in [Odds](#odds): daily-usable about 85 to 90%, full parity within the owner's [scope](#scope) about 60 to 65%, kernel independence about 10 to 15% without Shanling's source.

### Study hygiene

Rules for every agent and every brief from now on:

- Disassembly and decompiler output live outside every worktree (`~/.cache/q2-*-re/`, as the BlueZ and bluez-alsa studies already keep theirs) and are never pasted into the repository, an issue, a commit message, a pull request, a CI log or a release note. Ghidra's exports ([rebasing](rebasing.md#decompiling)) stay local, as today.
- What goes into the repository is the interface, in our own words and tables: names Shanling left in a symbol table, addresses, constants, the order of calls. No pseudo-C, no listings, no translated function bodies.
- New code is written from the interface note, not beside the listing. Where a behavior is subtle enough that the note would be a paraphrase of the code (a state machine's order, a table of register writes), capture it from the running system instead: the emulator's shim log, `strace`-style traces of a `--dev` Q2, D-Bus monitors. [player: Capturing](player.md#capturing) is the method.
- A stock file needed at run time (a module, a firmware blob) is never committed and, from M5, never shipped: it is named in a manifest by path and SHA-256, and copied on the device.

Seven services of ours is a ceiling, not a target: on 128 MB each process is about a megabyte before it does anything (inferred from `q2ui`'s 3.5 MB anonymous with a 480 KB canvas and a 9,198-row library). `q2sysd` is one process because its duties are small polls and writes that share one event loop; split it only if a duty needs another user.

### Process model

- **Init:** BusyBox `init`, as stock ([boot](boot.md#boot-sequence)), running a short `rcS`: mounts, `mdev`, the data partition, `/vendor`'s module loader ([Modules](#modules-under-a-new-init)), the splash, then **`runsvdir`** (BusyBox's runit applets) over `/etc/sv`. Each service is a directory with a `run` script: started in order, restarted when it dies, logged through the unified log. Not systemd (memory, boot time, no gain here) and not a supervisor of our own (BusyBox has one).
- **The UI is supervised like anything else.** Today a `demo` that ends reboots the Q2 ([boot](boot.md#rockbox-from-home)). In Vamp OS `q2ui` is restarted in place and comes back on its page from `q2playd`'s and its own saved state; three deaths in a minute start the rescue page ([Recovery](#recovery)).
- **IPC: Unix sockets of our own, one per service, under `/run/vamp/`.** Line-framed text with a versioned first line, the shape Q2SPOT/1 already has ([librespot](librespot.md#browsing-protocol-q2spot1)) and Car Remote's command queue uses. Reasons: the precedent works and is tested; a client needs no library (a shell script, Go and Rust speak it as they are); a socket file's owner, group and mode are the permission check, and `SO_PEERCRED` names the caller; and it can be exercised on a laptop with a pipe. Events are a subscription on the same socket, so nothing polls a file in `/tmp`.
- **D-Bus stays where upstream speaks it and nowhere else**: BlueZ, bluez-alsa and (if ever enabled) `wpa_supplicant`. `q2btd` is its only client of ours; an app never sees the system bus. This keeps `dbus-daemon`'s policy files few and keeps third-party code off a bus where BlueZ runs as root.
- **Files in `/tmp` as an interface are retired** (`/tmp/coda-rc.now`, `/tmp/q2-<id>.state`, `/tmp/q2mono` and the rest), each replaced by a call or an event, with the old file still written during the transition where an existing app reads it.

## Base system and build

### The choice: Buildroot, as an external tree

**Buildroot**, pinned to an LTS release by its tarball's SHA-256, with Vamp's packages and configuration in a `BR2_EXTERNAL` tree in the repository (`os/`). Reasons:

- Stock's rootfs is a Buildroot rootfs (read: its `os-release`, FFmpeg's configuration line, the layout), so the shape of the system is already this one, and every upstream piece Vamp OS needs has a maintained package: `bluez5_utils`, `bluez-alsa`, `wpa_supplicant`, `dbus`, `alsa-lib`, `mtd`, `squashfs`, BusyBox.
- It builds a root filesystem and nothing else: no kernel, no bootloader, no package manager on the target, which is exactly the part that is ours.
- `BR2_REPRODUCIBLE` and `make legal-info` exist: the second writes every package's license and source tarball, which is the GPL compliance record a release needs once the image is ours.
- `make sdk` produces a relocatable toolchain and sysroot, which is the SDK ([below](#sdk-and-simulator)).

Considered: **growing `tools/thirdparty.py`** into a whole system (it is a small Buildroot already: pinned sources, a keyed cache, an ABI check), rejected because the base needs about 40 packages with patches that Buildroot's maintainers already carry; **Yocto** (too heavy for one laptop and one small image); **OpenWrt** (a router's assumptions: its own init, its own bus, opkg; no Ingenic X2000 target either); **Alpine or a musl base** ([libc](#toolchain-and-libc)).

### Toolchain and libc

- **ABI: fixed by the hardware and the kernel, and kept.** MIPS32r2 little-endian, o32, hard float with a 64-bit FPU, NaN 2008 (`-EL -march=mips32r2 -mabi=32 -mhard-float -mfp64 -mnan=2008 -mabs=2008`, read: [third-party](third-party.md#toolchain)), interpreter `ld-linux-mipsn8.so.1`. Every Vamp program and every card app in the wild is built to it. Whether the X2000's FPU would run legacy-NaN code at all is not known (`/proc/cpuinfo` and the kernel's `ieee754=` setting, a read on a Q2, would say) and nothing needs it to.
- **libc: glibc.** musl has no named ABI for NaN 2008 before MIPS release 6 (from memory of its supported targets; check before relying on it), BlueZ and GLib are tested against glibc, existing apps link glibc's `libc.so.6`, and the size musl would save is small beside the 30 MB that leave with `demo` and its assets.
- **Two steps, so that one thing changes at a time.**
  1. **External toolchain: Codescape 2020.06** (GCC 9.3, glibc 2.28), the one stock was built with and `tools/thirdparty.py` already fetches. The first Vamp OS image then runs the same libc build `demo` and today's helpers link, which is what the transition needs while `demo` still runs ([M5](#m5-the-vamp-os-base-and-the-installer)).
  2. **Buildroot's own toolchain** once `demo` is off: a current GCC and glibc with the same ABI flags, kernel headers set to 5.10 (the libc must not assume a system call the kernel lacks), and 64-bit `time_t` for our own programs. glibc keeps old symbol versions, so apps built against 2.28 keep running (inferred from glibc's compatibility rule; the conformance suite checks it for the API's own library).
- **The kernel's constraints on userspace**: headers no newer than 5.10; whatever it was configured without. Unknown and worth one read-only session on a Q2 (`/proc/config.gz` if present, else `/proc/filesystems`, `/proc/cgroups`, `/proc/self/ns`, `/proc/devices`, `/proc/sys/kernel/seccomp`): devtmpfs, loop devices, cgroups, namespaces, seccomp, netfilter, zram, inotify. The isolation design depends on the answers ([Permissions](#permissions-and-isolation)).

### Modules under a new init

- The modules are loaded by path with `insmod`, each from its own short script that carries its parameters (pins, bus numbers, the framebuffer's `layer0_frames=2`), in the order `driver_default_init_script.sh` lists (read: the stock rootfs; [boot](boot.md#boot-sequence), [hardware](hardware.md)). No `modprobe`, no `depmod`, no `/lib/modules`. The kernel does not change, so every module's version check passes as it does today.
- Vamp OS's `rcS` runs that script from `/vendor/module_driver` unchanged, with `/module_driver` and `/firmware` as symlinks for the paths the scripts, the Wi-Fi driver and `rtk_hciattach` name. Running Shanling's scripts as they are (they are vendor files too, copied with the modules) means a parameter of theirs is never retyped by us.
- Later, optional, and a daily gain of its own: load only what the boot needs first (framebuffer, panel, keys, wheel, DAC) and the radio driver when a radio is turned on, which is what Standby already does in reverse. The radio driver is 3.3 MB of the 4.6 MB of modules and takes about 10 s of the boot's background (read: [hardware: Wi-Fi faults](hardware.md#wi-fi-faults)).
- The MCU's firmware (`libmcu-bare.bin`, 16 KB) is loaded at boot by a stock tool; the load becomes a call in `q2sysd` once traced, and the file is a vendor file until Vamp writes its own MCU program ([backlog](backlog.md), speaker sounds).

## Installer and updates

### What stays Shanling's

The **vendor set**, about 5.2 MB (read from the stock rootfs): the 24 modules and their 25 scripts in `/module_driver`, `/firmware/rtlbt/rtl8733bs_fw` and `rtl8733bs_config`, `/firmware/rtlwifi/PHY_REG_PG.txt` and `TXPWR_LMT.txt`, and `/usr/data/libmcu-bare.bin`; `rtk_hciattach` and `cmd_mcu` join it until each has a replacement checked on a Q2. During the transition (M5 to M7) a second, temporary set rides with it: `demo`, `libawtk.so`, `libtkc.so` and the Shanling libraries `demo` links, about 20 MB.

In flash and never rewritten: U-Boot, the kernel, the recovery's kernel and rootfs, `userid`.

Unavoidable, with reasons: the modules are built for this exact kernel and their source is not published; the radio's firmware and tables are Realtek's and Shanling's calibration; the kernel is the one thing the modules load into. Nothing else.

### How an image is made on the device

A Vamp OS release is `vampos.squashfs` (ours and open source), its signature, and a manifest. The installer, `q2pkgd`'s system half, runs on the Q2:

1. **Verify** the release's signature (Ed25519, the release key, as today: [internals](internals.md#release-signatures)).
2. **Collect the vendor set** from the running root filesystem. Each file is checked against the manifest's table of known SHA-256s per stock version (hashes are facts about files; the files are not shipped). A file with an unknown hash stops the install with its name: an unsupported stock version, or a damaged file.
3. **Compose**: append `/vendor` to a copy of `vampos.squashfs` on the card with `mksquashfs`'s append mode, single-threaded and memory-capped (the vendor set is 5 MB; the copy is the slow part).
4. **Check the result** against the manifest's expected hash for that stock version. A match means the image about to be flashed is byte for byte one that was built and tested before release. A mismatch stops the install.
5. **Hand to the recovery**: write the image as `recovery-update/rootfs.squashfs` on the card, mark `mtd6` as stock's updater does (`ota:kernel2`), restart. Shanling's recovery, in flash, erases `mtd2` and writes the image. Using their recovery rather than a flasher of ours is deliberate: its marker survives a power cut mid-write, so the Q2 comes back into the recovery, not into half a root filesystem.

What this needs that is not known yet, all from one session with a Q2 (read `mtd3` and `mtd4` with `nanddump`, study them privately):

- **What the recovery requires on the card.** Stock's updater in `demo` checks both `xImage` and `rootfs.squashfs`; whether the recovery itself needs a kernel image, and whether it verifies anything, is not known. If it needs one, the installer writes the Q2's own kernel back to it, read from `mtd1` (the user's own copy, never leaving the device), which rewrites the kernel partition with the bytes it already holds.
- **Whether `mksquashfs` composes in 128 MB** at an acceptable speed with the player stopped, and reproducibly (above).
- **Whether the kernel has loop devices.** If it does, a simpler layout is possible (the vendor set as its own small squashfs, mounted at `/vendor`), kept as the fallback design if step 3 disappoints. A plain copy of the vendor set on `/mnt/data` is the last resort: it puts the screen's driver on writable flash.

## Platform

Vamp OS is a product and a base. The base's promise to someone who is not us: **a documented, versioned API; a package format with a manifest and permissions; an SDK and a simulator that need no file of Shanling's; and extension points that Vamp's own features use too.** The last is the rule that keeps the rest honest: Vamp's bundled apps (Subsonic today) and as many system pages as practical are built on the public API, so a gap in it is our problem first.

### The API

Version 1 of the Vamp API is a set of service sockets, each with a protocol document in [`docs/platform/`](platform/README.md), a conformance test that runs against the real service on a laptop, and a small C library (`libvamp`, not written yet) that is a convenience, not a requirement.

| Area            | Service            | What an app can do                                                                                                                                       | Grows from                                                                                                                   |
| --------------- | ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| UI, declarative | `q2ui`             | Serve lists, rows, actions, search, fields, progress, QR and hold menus that the system draws in its own look, with art and live refresh                 | Q2SPOT/1 and its capability words ([librespot](librespot.md#browsing-protocol-q2spot1), [internals](internals.md#card-apps)) |

**Versioning and stability.** Each protocol opens with its name and major version (`VAMP/1 playback`). Within a major version changes are additive, and a client learns what a service has from capability words, the mechanism the card apps already use so that "apps need no change, and older firmware shows the row" ([internals](internals.md#card-apps)). A package's manifest states the lowest API it needs. A major version, once released, is served for as long as an app in the signed list uses it. **Q2SPOT/1 is API 0 and is served for good**, by a shim over the new services, so every app that exists today runs unchanged. The API is marked experimental until two apps written outside the project ship on it; that, not a date, is when 1.0 is declared.

### App model and packages

Today a card app is a folder `.<id>/` on the card with a `q2app` file of `key=value` lines, a `run` launcher and a binary, installed from a release zip that the signed `sources.list` pins by SHA-256 ([internals](internals.md#q2-shelf)). That is a package format already; Vamp OS keeps its shape and removes its limits.

- **The package** is the zip, with the `q2app` manifest extended: `api=` (the lowest API version), `version=`, `kind=` (`browse`, `toolkit`, `game`, `service`, `theme`, `language`, `provider`), `entry=` (the program, started with arguments, no shell), `permissions=`, and the declarations for its [extension points](#extension-points). A manifest with none of the new keys is an API 0 app and is run as today.
- **Kinds.** A `browse` app serves pages the system draws: the cheapest to write, the most consistent, and what Subsonic, Deezer, Soulseek and the rest are. A `toolkit` app draws with `libvampui`. A `game` takes the canvas, or is a game for the built-in engine ([games-engine](games-engine.md)). A `service` runs in the background under the supervisor with a declared reason to be awake (a scrobbler, a sync). A `theme` or `language` is data only.
- **Trust.** The store keeps the model that works: the maintainer's key signs a list that pins each exact zip, so no repository's owner can change what a Q2 installs ([internals](internals.md#q2-shelf)). What changes: more than one list (a user can add a source, with its own key and a plain warning), and **developer mode**, off by default, which installs an unsigned package from the card or over USB and shows its permissions first.
- **The App Store** becomes a page of `q2ui` over `q2pkgd`, listing every kind, with what each package may do. The cap of 16 entries and the 8-folder scan go; so does the copy of each binary into RAM, where the kernel allows running from the card under the app's user.

### Permissions and isolation

Today every program on the Q2 is root. Vamp OS's floor, which needs nothing the kernel might lack:

- **A user per package**, made at install. Its files on `/mnt/data` are its own.
- **A group per permission** (`playback`, `library`, `library-write`, `settings`, `network-hold`, `screen`, `music-files`, `background`), and each service's socket, or each call on it, checks the caller's groups through `SO_PEERCRED`. A permission not in the manifest is a call refused, with a line in the log.
- **No device nodes.** An app reaches the screen and the input through `q2ui` and audio through `q2playd`; the framebuffer and ALSA devices belong to system users. A `game` holding the canvas is given the framebuffer by the broker for that time, under the `screen` permission.
- **Limits**: address space and process counts by `setrlimit`, and an OOM score that makes an app the first to go and the player the last.

What depends on the kernel's configuration, and so on the read-only session listed under [Toolchain and libc](#toolchain-and-libc): memory limits per app (cgroups), a private view of the card (mount namespaces; without them the card is FAT or exFAT with no owners, so `music-files` is all or nothing), system-call filters (seccomp), and denying the network to an app (network namespaces or netfilter's owner match; without either, network access cannot be withheld, and the store says so plainly). The design uses what is there and documents what is not, rather than promising a sandbox the kernel cannot hold.

### SDK and simulator

- **The SDK** is Buildroot's `make sdk` output, published with each release: the cross toolchain, the sysroot with `libvamp` and `libvampui`, and the protocol documents. Go (`GOARCH=mipsle GOMIPS=hardfloat`) and Rust apps need only the documents, as today's do.
- **The simulator**: `q2ui` and every service already build for the host for their tests (`tools/ui.py`, `tools/player.py`, the pattern of `test/player`). Packaged together with a fake board behind `q2sysd` and a window for the screen and wheel, that is a Vamp OS that runs natively on a laptop, on which an app's host build runs too. The QEMU emulator ([building](building.md#emulator-pool)) remains for MIPS binaries; with an image that holds no stock file it can be given to anyone, which today's cannot.
- **The workflow**: `vamp new` (a template per kind), `vamp run` (in the simulator), `vamp pack`, `vamp install` (to a Q2 in developer mode over adb or Wi-Fi), `vamp check` (the conformance tests against the app's side of a protocol). `tools/vamp.py`, published with the SDK.
- **A board file.** Everything hardware-specific sits behind `q2sysd`'s backends and a vendor manifest. The Shanling M1 Plus runs the same `demo`, AWTK and `hciplayer` stack ([backlog](backlog.md), server library); a second board is the test that the foundation is one. Not planned, but the layout must not prevent it.

## Milestones

Each is shippable on its own and has a switch back. Order: what the owner uses every day first, then the foundation for others, then the system underneath. "Q2" marks work that needs a Q2 on a desk.

### M0. The three spikes land (in flight)

`q2ui`'s engine behind `--ui-spike` and `/mnt/data/ui-target` (`feat/render-engine`, toolkit step in progress), upstream `bluetoothd` 5.88 behind `--own-bluez` (`feat/bluez-own`, built and tested under `qemu-mipsel`), upstream bluez-alsa 5.0.0 behind its switch (`feat/bluealsa-own`, spike build in progress). Default builds are unchanged. **Switch back:** nothing to switch; all off by default. **Q2:** the panel's refresh rate and depth (render-engine's three questions), `docs/bluez.md`'s device plan steps 0 to 3 and 9 to 10.

### M1. The new UI where you live

Home, the Library's lists and Now Playing in `q2ui`, with `demo` running as a service behind the screen hand-over and the command queue (`docs/ui-rewrite.md`, migration steps 1 to 3). The biggest daily gain there is: a nineteenth of the CPU per tick, gliding rows, tvOS-style focus, no idle wake-ups. **Switch back:** a Settings row and a held key choose `demo`'s UI for a boot, as today's Rockbox choice works. Wireless adb (adbd over Wi-Fi with key auth and mDNS) and the first `vamp` device commands (find, connect, shell, push, screenshot, logs) land here, the owner's priority, since every later milestone is tested through them. **Q2:** frame pacing and dropped frames, memory with both programs resident, a week of the owner's use.

### M2. Bluetooth from source

Upstream `bluetoothd` and `bluealsa` the default, Shanling's hooks ported as source, `q2btd` begun as the home of what `libbtctl.so` does where `demo` no longer needs to. Lands beside M1, on other workers. **Switch back:** the launcher's key and `q2guard`'s automatic fallback to stock's daemons (`docs/bluez.md`, Cutover). **Q2:** nearly all of it: real headsets, codecs, reconnection, Car Remote over BLE, a week without a fallback.

### M3. Platform v0: the foundation, usable by others

On today's base, before the system underneath changes: `q2playd` takes the player's socket and the queue from `demo` (migration step 5, moved up because a platform needs a playback service with more than one client); the Vamp API 1 documents and conformance tests for UI (declarative and toolkit), playback, library reads, settings and input; `libvampui`; the manifest's new keys, package users and permission groups; developer mode; the simulator, the SDK and `vamp`; Subsonic moved onto API 1 as the proof. Existing apps run on the API 0 shim. **Switch back:** `demo`'s UI still owns everything it did at M1 when chosen; API 0 apps never notice. **Q2:** little: package users on the real card, developer-mode install over adb.

### M4. Settings and Vamp's features on `q2ui`

Settings as tables, Cover Flow, Search, the EQ page, Games, the app launcher and the App Store page, Radio, the status row's indicators, every card and dialog (migration steps 4 and 6). Each page moved is deleted from the payload in the same change. At the end `demo` draws nothing. **Switch back:** per page while it exists in both; the boot choice until the last page moves, after which the fallback is the previous release. **Q2:** spot checks per batch; the long tail of dialogs under real cables and headsets.

### M5. The Vamp OS base and the installer

Started early, beside M1, as a clean-slate image with no `demo` at all: [vamp-os-base](vamp-os-base.md) (the Buildroot tree, its init, the vendor set's compose, and what has and has not been shown under the emulator).

The Buildroot image, `runsvdir`, the layout, `/vendor`, the installer and the rescue page; `demo` and its libraries carried as the temporary legacy set and patched on the device. The bridge release ships first. **From this release no Vamp artifact contains a file of Shanling's or the kernel**, and Vamp's GPL code is no longer distributed joined to `demo`. **Switch back:** the previous image on the card; a classic `update.tar`; stock. **Q2:** the whole milestone is bring-up: the recovery's contract, composing on the device, first boot under the new init, every module loaded, suspend and resume. A second Q2 kept on a known image would halve the cost of each mistake.

### M6. The services

One at a time, each behind its own switch that leaves the duty with `demo`, each traced first: `q2libd` (the scan and index), `q2netd` (Wi-Fi, time, the DLNA renderer), `q2sysd`'s duties in order of risk (settings, battery, jack and DAC, screen and Standby, USB modes, the MCU), `q2pkgd`'s update path without `demo`'s dialog. M5 and M6 can overlap: they share only the desk. **Switch back:** per service, a key that hands the duty back to `demo`, until M7. **Q2:** all of it.

### M7. `demo` off: Vamp OS 1.0

The legacy set is dropped from the installer; `HOOKS`, the payload, `tools/ipod.py`'s asset rewrites, the rebasing tables and `test/patch.py` leave the tree; the toolchain moves to Buildroot's own with 64-bit time; the parity checklist is signed against [Scope](#scope), with stock's other built-in services (QQ Music, NetEase, Baidu, SyncLink) as its deviations. The image is about 30 MB smaller. **The combination of GPL code and `demo` no longer exists anywhere.** **Switch back:** the last M6 release, today's Vamp through the bridge, stock. **Q2:** a release candidate's week.

### M8. Kernel independence (conditional)

Only with Shanling's kernel source, or a mainline port someone else carries: a 5.10 LTS with the vendor drivers rebuilt, then the modules from source where source exists. Not scheduled; [Odds](#odds).

## Risks

Ranked by likelihood times cost.

1. **Hardware interfaces only a Q2 shows.** Standby and resume, the DAC's power order (a wrong one is a pop in someone's ears), the jack's quirk, USB roles and charging. The emulator fakes or lacks each. Mitigation: trace before writing, one duty at a time, `demo` kept as the per-service fallback through M6.
2. **Parity's long tail, and two UIs for months.** Ninety pages hold behavior nobody listed. Mitigation: the checklist built page by page beside `demo` in the emulator; a moved page deleted from the payload at once; deviations written down and signed.
3. **The installer.** An on-device compose and a recovery whose contract is not yet read. A mistake is a Q2 that does not boot. Mitigation: the expected-hash check before any flash; Shanling's recovery for the write; the key-combination recovery that needs nothing of ours; the bridge release soaked on the owner's Q2 first.
4. **Study hygiene.** One pasted listing in a commit or a CI log is a publication, and the project rests on Shanling's goodwill (their Q2 page praises it). Mitigation: [the rules above](#study-hygiene) in every brief; a pre-commit and CI grep for decompiler signatures (`FUN_`, `undefined4`, `DAT_`, `param_1`) as a backstop.
5. **Memory during the transition.** 128 MB, no swap; `demo` is 41 MB resident, and M1 to M6 run it beside `q2ui` and the new services. Mitigation: measure on a Q2 before each milestone ships; services start on demand; `demo`'s pages freed as they move.
6. **Bluetooth on a 2021 kernel with a 2026 daemon**, the LE attribute channel that Car Remote owns today, and behavior Shanling changed without leaving a string (`docs/bluez.md`, Risks). Mitigation: the device plan, and the automatic fallback.
7. **Codecs and names.** aptX, LDAC and AAC encoders built from source are a licensing and patent question separate from this plan's copyright one; shipping them is today's practice only because the stock binaries are in the image. Needs its own review before M2 is the default.
8. **The API frozen too early, or the platform starving the product.** Mitigation: API 1 covers only what Vamp's own apps use; experimental until two outside apps ship; M3 is sized at about a tenth of the whole.
9. **Fonts and art.** `q2ui`'s fallback face for scripts Inter lacks is stock's font today (inferred from `ui/main.c`'s `default.ttf`); stock's icons and strings cannot ship in an image of ours. An open CJK face is large for a 72 MiB image and needs subsetting; the OLED theme's icons are already Phosphor's ([third-party](third-party.md#artwork)).
10. **The kernel, for good.** Its Bluetooth and Wi-Fi bugs stay reachable ([third-party](third-party.md#what-reaches-them)), and what it was built without bounds the isolation the platform can offer.
11. **Build weight.** A Buildroot build is hours cold and tens of gigabytes on a 16 GB laptop whose disk has filled before. Mitigation: one build directory on the scratch disk, a cache keyed as `tools/thirdparty.py`'s is, the merge queue building only changed packages.
12. **Shanling changes course**: a stock update that locks the updater or the recovery, or a request to stop. The on-device design is the answer to the second as far as a design can be.

## Odds

Judgments, with what they rest on.

- **(a) Daily-usable (M1 and M2 as the owner's defaults): 85 to 90%.** The UI's hard part is measured on the device already, the hand-over reuses two gates the payload has, and Bluetooth's replacement builds and passes 45 of 47 checks off the device (bluez's STATUS, first run). What is left to chance is Bluetooth on real headsets, and its fallback is automatic.
- **(b) Full parity (M7), meaning everything [Scope](#scope) keeps: about 60 to 65%.** The scope ruling helps: the features dropped were the ones with the least source of ours behind them. `q2player` got there, at a fifteenth of the size. The doubt is not any one piece but the sum: about 200 items and 50 desk sessions with one owner's attention, and a firmware that is already good at each step, which is the usual place for a rewrite to rest.
- **(c) Kernel independence: 10 to 15%** as things stand. Mainline Linux has no complete X2000 support known to this plan (not checked for this page), the vendor modules have no published source, and a kernel that boots but lacks the DAC or the radio is not a product. With Shanling's source in hand: 50 to 60%, bounded then by the modules whose source is not theirs to give.
