# Bluetooth connectivity

What limits the Q2's Bluetooth, layer by layer, what the logs show about the dropouts owners hear, how to measure it on a Q2 without disturbing it, and what is worth changing. The firmware's own Bluetooth code is in [internals](internals.md): [Bluetooth AAC](internals.md#bluetooth-aac), [Bluetooth link monitor](internals.md#bluetooth-link-monitor) (the q2-bt log this page reads, and since 2026-11-25 the sampler below, as its recorder), [Bluetooth coexistence pacing](internals.md#bluetooth-coexistence-pacing), [Bluetooth controller watchdog](internals.md#bluetooth-controller-watchdog), [Bluetooth devices](internals.md#bluetooth-devices), [Smart Wi-Fi](internals.md#smart-wi-fi). The radio's hardware is in [hardware: Radio and antennas](hardware.md#radio-and-antennas), and its firmware versions in [firmware: Bluetooth controller](firmware.md#bluetooth-controller).

From a study of 2026-11-25: the link monitor's logs of 2026-11-21 to 25 (16,390 per-second lines, 12,767 s of AAC), the event log, one read of the coex state, and the stock binaries. Each figure says whether it was measured on a Q2, read from code or binaries, quoted, inferred or estimated.

## What limits it

Most of what is heard is not a weak stack in the audio path; the radio does fall short in bursts, and the reasons are mostly outside the firmware's audio code.

- **The audio path is not dropping packets.** bluealsa, the player and BlueZ kept up throughout: no encoder stall, bluealsa at 13.8% of a core on AAC (measured).
- **The audible break-ups were 41 link-stall seconds** (0.3% of the AAC time) and 167 s with the A2DP socket backed up, in four clusters (measured, `tools/q2bt.py`): the Q2's own Wi-Fi downloading on 2.4 GHz, outside 2.4 GHz interference, and one-second body or hand dips. See [The dropouts of 2026-11-24](#the-dropouts-of-2026-11-24).
- **In a bad band even AAC does not fit.** In the seconds every ACL buffer was held, the controller delivered a median of 40 to 43 packets a second of about 490 to 600 bytes (at most 80 to 91), about 200 kbps, against the roughly 46 packets a second AAC at 220 kbps needs (measured). AAC has no lower rate today, and a headset with 150 ms of buffer (`delay=150.0` to `156.7 ms` reported, measured) hears any stall longer than that.
- **The three supervision-timeout losses on record came on an idle, paused link**, not mid-stream: two the Q2 sitting on a cable while the headset went out of range, one the headset ending the link itself (measured; classified by inference). None was a controller or H5 fault.
- **Hard limits** nothing in software passes: a fixed 4 dBm transmit power and no power control seen (measured); EDR 2 Mbps only (code: `rtl8733bs_config` clears 3 Mbps); no LE Audio on kernel 5.10 ([hardware](hardware.md)); the Bluetooth antenna on the battery's face under the plastic back (the teardown photo); Realtek's closed H5 driver and controller firmware, where the UART storms live (code); Shanling's unpublished kernel and BlueZ changes ([third-party](third-party.md#not-replaced)).

## Coexistence

The RTL8733BS is one 2.4 GHz radio with two antennas, and Realtek's coex in the Wi-Fi driver (`halbtcoutsrc`, the 8733B tables) shares the band: TDMA cycles that give each side time, coex tables, and an "AFH Map to BT" that keeps Bluetooth off the Wi-Fi channel. Its module parameters (`rtw_btcoex_enable`, `rtw_ant_num`, `rtw_single_ant_path`, `rtw_ips_mode`, `rtw_lps_level`, `rtw_power_mgnt`) are not passed by stock's `module_driver/rtl8733bs.sh`, so the efuse decides. The Bluetooth side reports its profiles through `rtk_coex.c`.

`cat /proc/net/rtl8733bs/wlan0/btcoex` prints the whole state. Read on 2026-11-25, Wi-Fi joined on 2.4 GHz and idle, Bluetooth connected and idle:

| `Ver Coex/ Para/ BT_Dez/ BT_Rpt`      | `20260527_39/ 20241204_0f/ 0x0c/ 0x0e (Match)`                    | the driver's coex code and parameter table; `BT_Dez` is the lowest Bluetooth coex version the driver wants, `BT_Rpt` what the controller firmware reports; Match means 0x0e meets 0x0c (inferred from the field names and Realtek's convention) |

What follows from it:

- **5 GHz takes Wi-Fi off Bluetooth's band.** On 5 GHz Realtek's coex turns TDMA off (Linux `rtw88`'s `rtw_coex_action_wl_under5g`: TDMA case 0 shared, 100 non-shared), so Bluetooth has 2.4 GHz to itself except while Wi-Fi scans it. Which band the Q2 was on at each complaint is not logged; at that read it was 2.4 GHz.
- **Which TDMA case a stream gets is not known**: the read was with no profile running. A dump during an A2DP stream on 2.4 GHz (the recorder's `COEX` lines, every minute while a link is up) shows it.
- **Already held:** wpa_supplicant's 5 s rescans while unjoined, and `scan_interval 3600` while A2DP plays ([Smart Wi-Fi](internals.md#smart-wi-fi)).
- **Starvation in the link log** looks like `wq` above 0 with `fly` at `bufs` and `done` under `acl` while `rssi` holds steady, with `wtx` or `wrx` busy. `afh` falling is the environment, not the Q2's Wi-Fi (coex's own map removes about 21 channels around a 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi channel, inferred).
- **The H5 storms and Wi-Fi power states** have no consistent link: one storm came with Wi-Fi in IPS, another 23 s after a DHCP lease (measured). More storms are needed.
- The H5 port read `tx:117194 rx:31613 fe:2 pe:3 brk:2` with RTS/CTS that boot (measured): framing, parity and break errors and no overrun, the storms' signature, with no storm.

## The dropouts of 2026-11-24

**The three link losses** (supervision timeout, HCI reason 1 or the headset's own end). The link monitor wrote per-second lines then only while A2DP streamed (it records idle links too since 2026-11-25: [A day-long sampler](#a-day-long-sampler)), and none ran at any of them, so there is no RSSI trend before any; what is known is from the event log and the unified log. At the stream start after each, the H5 port's counters read `fe=0 pe=0 brk=0 oe=0 bo=0`, and no H5 or `tx timeout` line came near any of them (measured).

| Loss | Before                                                                                              | After                                                                                                                      | Reading                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ---- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | headset paused from the headset; stream stopped; the Q2 put on a USB cable 2 min before, screen off | the Q2's page timed out; the headset reconnected by itself 6 min later                                                     | range: a Q2 on a charger, a paused headset carried away and back (inferred, medium)                                                                                          |
| 2    | headset paused 20 min before; the Q2 still on the cable                                             | page timed out; the headset reconnected by itself 4 min later, and the cable was pulled 47 s after that                    | range, the same pattern (inferred, medium)                                                                                                                                   |
| 3    | headset paused 57 min before, after a stall cluster; off the cable; screen off                      | BlueZ's own reconnect succeeded 24 s later; the headset paused and ended the link itself 30 s after that (HCI reason 0x14) | headset-side: reachable, then it chose to leave, as AirPods do moving to another Apple device or into the case (inferred, low to medium); a brief body block is not excluded |

None is coexistence: an idle ACL link exchanges a poll every few tens of ms, and a supervision timeout needs none answered for its whole length (BlueZ's default is 20 s), while TDMA case 3 leaves Bluetooth 44 to 45 ms in every cycle (inferred, high).

## Measuring on a Q2

Over adb ([adb](adb.md)) or `q2remote`. `hcitool con` gives each link's handle `H`; the commands below take it as two bytes, low first (handle 1 is `0x01 0x00`). `MAC` is the headset's address from `hcitool con`.

**No traffic to the controller** (files and ioctls):

```sh
date -u; cat /proc/uptime
hciconfig hci0                                  # byte, ACL and error counters
hcitool con                                     # links, handles, role (MASTER or SLAVE)
cat /proc/net/l2cap                             # Wmem: the A2DP socket's queue
grep '^0:' '/proc/tty/driver/INGENIC serial'    # the H5 port: tx rx fe pe brk oe bo
cat /proc/net/wireless; cat /sys/class/net/wlan0/statistics/rx_bytes /sys/class/net/wlan0/statistics/tx_bytes
wpa_cli -i wlan0 status | grep -E '^(freq|wpa_state)='; wpa_cli -i wlan0 signal_poll
cat /proc/net/rtl8733bs/wlan0/ps_info
dbus-send --system --print-reply --dest=org.bluealsa /org/bluealsa org.bluealsa.Manager1.GetPCMs
```

**HCI reads**, the class `q2guard`'s link monitor sends (RSSI and the AFH map each second while streaming and every 10 s while a link is up; the role, the two timeouts and the maximum power once a link: [A day-long sampler](#a-day-long-sampler)):

```sh
hcitool rssi MAC; hcitool lq MAC; hcitool tpl MAC; hcitool afh MAC
hcitool cmd 0x02 0x0009 HL HH        # Role Discovery
hcitool cmd 0x03 0x0036 HL HH        # Read Link Supervision Timeout (units of 0.625 ms)
hcitool cmd 0x03 0x0027 HL HH        # Read Automatic Flush Timeout
hcitool cmd 0x02 0x000c HL HH        # Read Link Policy Settings
hcitool cmd 0x05 0x0001 HL HH        # Read Failed Contact Counter
hcitool cmd 0x03 0x002d HL HH 0x01   # Read Transmit Power Level, maximum (never yet read on a Q2)
hciconfig hci0 version; hciconfig hci0 features
```

**Driver-to-firmware queries** (at most once a minute):

```sh
cat /proc/net/rtl8733bs/wlan0/btcoex
cat /proc/net/rtl8733bs/wlan0/rx_signal /proc/net/rtl8733bs/wlan0/trx_info /proc/net/rtl8733bs/wlan0/ap_info
mount -t debugfs none /sys/kernel/debug 2>/dev/null
cat /sys/kernel/debug/bluetooth/hci0/idle_timeout /sys/kernel/debug/bluetooth/hci0/sniff_min_interval /sys/kernel/debug/bluetooth/hci0/sniff_max_interval
```

**Never while diagnosing:** `hcitool scan` or `inq` (an inquiry came before 3 of 9 earlier storms), `hciconfig hci0 reset`, `up` or `down`, `hcitool cc`, `dc`, `sr` or `cpt`, and any write to `btcoex` or the driver's `coex_force_*` files.

### A day-long sampler

The link monitor logged nothing while the link was idle, and that is where the losses came. The read-only sampler the study designed is **built into the link monitor** since 2026-11-25, as its recorder (`patch/q2bt.c`, [internals: Bluetooth link monitor](internals.md#bluetooth-link-monitor), **The recorder**), rather than the study's shell script: q2guard already held the HCI sockets, the rate limits and the log. It runs whenever q2guard does, release builds included, and writes to the same q2-bt log:

- **Every second** while a classic link is up and no stream runs, with no controller traffic: `IDLE` with the HCI byte counters each way, Wi-Fi's bytes, the largest L2CAP `Wmem` and the H5 port's error counts (`ue=fe/pe/brk/oe/bo`); a stream's own line carries the same, `brx` and `ue` added to it.
- **Every 10 s per classic link:** `ACL` with RSSI and the AFH map (two HCI reads; the streaming link's from its own line).
- **Once per link:** `ACLINFO` with the role, supervision timeout, flush timeout and maximum transmit power; `ACL gone` when it leaves.
- **Every 60 s** while a link is up: `WIFI` with wpa_supplicant's frequency, band, channel and state, and as `COEX` lines the sixteen `btcoex` lines that move (Wi-Fi channel, band and traffic, power state, throughput, `BT status`, profiles, role, the BT side's AFH map, `TDMA`, `Coex_Mode`, `Null All`, `Cnt TDMA_Togg`, `BT slot avg`, `0x770`, `0x774`, `Path Owner`) rather than the whole 4 KB. `ps_info`'s power-save counts are in the `UART` notes at each START and storm.
- **Not sampled**, as they never moved in 16,390 lines: `lq` (always 0), `tpl` (always 4), `err` and `fcc` (0), and `ew`, `pr`, `pd`, `pw` (never readable on this kernel). They have left the stream's line too, so it asks two HCI commands a second instead of five.
- The card's `.q2muse/logs/q2-bt.0` to `.3`, 1 MB each, the link monitor's rotation: about 0.68 MB an hour streaming and 0.32 MB idle (measured under the test harness), so five to six hours. `tools/q2bt.py` prints each link's RSSI in its last minute before it went.

Before trusting it, prove each field moves: toggle Wi-Fi (the `WIFI` line's `freq` and the `COEX` band line change), walk a paused headset out of range for 30 s (the `ACL` lines' RSSI falls, then `ACL gone`), and check `ue` holds at 0 through a normal stream.

| Q2 Muse's software    | the link clean (`done` equal to `acl`, `wq` 0) but the source stalling (`er` falling with the rate, `pl` or `dk` high), player or bluealsa errors, a PCM busy, the transport idle while playing, `BT RESET` or H5 lines | already in the logs; match the owner's noted times                                                                                        |

In order: the owner's noted dropout times against the logs; one no-traffic and HCI snapshot during normal listening and one within a minute of a dropout; the sampler for a day; then A/B tests one variable at a time (AirPods switching, Wi-Fi band, posture, AAC against SBC on one headset), each with the owner's UTC times of what was heard.
